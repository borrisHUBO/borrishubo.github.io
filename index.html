I work with Hanseul Yang at KAIST on problems that sit between evolution and medicine.

I study how traits evolve across animal genomes, especially where distant species have independently arrived at the same solution, and I use systems biology and model organisms to work out which of these can be carried over to humans. My current focus is the evolutionary origin of complex regeneration. A few animals, including the spiny mouse (Acomys), can rebuild tissue that most mammals only scar. I am tracing how that ability arose, and using Acomys and other regenerative species to study how it works.

I am completing my PhD at Seoul National University, funded by the Hyundai Motor Chung Mong-Koo Foundation and previously supported by the Global Korea Scholarship (GKS) of NIIED. I welcome conversations with biotech and pharmaceutical teams working on related problems, and email is the best way to reach me.
